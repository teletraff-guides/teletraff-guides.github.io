The panel's modules work with other people's accounts, other people's channels and personal data.
Below are the boundaries worth settling **before** a launch rather than after the first complaint.

## Recipient consent

Messaging, inviting and work in direct messages affect specific people.

- Write to those who expect to hear from you: customers, subscribers, members of your community.
- Presence in a collected dataset **is not consent** to receive messages.
- Leave a clear way to opt out of further messages, and honour it.
- Data-protection and marketing law applies regardless of what the panel can technically do.

## Platform rules

Telegram's rules take precedence over any panel setting. Bulk actions can lead to an account being
restricted — that is the platform's decision, not the panel's.

<Aside type="caution" title="What the panel does not do">
TeleTraff does not bypass Telegram's restrictions. If an account is restricted, comments in a channel
are closed or a user forbids being added to groups — the panel shows that in the results rather than
forcing through.
</Aside>

## Other people's venues

- Commenting and taking part in chats are acceptable where the venue owner does not object to such
  activity.
- Open comments do not mean consent to automated replies.
- The rules of the specific community take precedence over your settings.

## Reports

The [report log](/en/modules/reporting/) is meant for submissions about genuine violations. Mass
reports against legitimate resources are abuse that harms the sender's own accounts first.

## Responsibility

Responsibility for which modules are running, against which dataset and with what content lies with
the operator. An instruction to a language model does not remove that: text sent from your account
remains your text.
