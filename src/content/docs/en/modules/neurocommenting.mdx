AI commenting leaves comments under **new** posts of the channels you select, from your accounts.
You set the sources, the post-selection rule and the instruction for the text — the panel watches
for publications and comments on the matching ones.

Below is the working launch sequence. The module overview lives on the
[AI commenting page](https://teletraff.com/en/modules/neurocommenting).

## What you need before launch

- **Warmed-up accounts.** Telegram restricts fresh and empty ones fastest — start with
  [warmup](/en/modules/account-warmup/).
- **A proxy on every account.** Without one the account will not run — see the
  [proxy centre](/en/modules/proxy-center/).
- **Access to a language model** if the text is generated: the panel's shared provider or your own key.
- **Channels with open comments.** If discussions are disabled, the channel is skipped.

## How to launch

<Steps>

1. **Select accounts and target channels.**

   One account runs one thread and watches a limited number of channels at a time — the panel shows
   the cap during selection. If there are more channels than your accounts can carry, the extras
   simply will not be picked up: add accounts rather than raising the pace on the current ones.

2. **Set the post-selection rule.**

   | Mode | What gets commented |
   | --- | --- |
   | All matching | every new post from the source |
   | By keywords | only posts containing your words or phrases |
   | Random share | a share of new posts, the proportion is yours to set |

3. **Choose the text source.**

   **AI** — the connected model writes by your instruction. **Templates** — your pre-written text
   is sent, no model needed. **Mixed** — the type is chosen by a set proportion and recorded
   in the result.

4. **Check the thread distribution.**

   The panel builds a plan in advance: how many targets and actions fall to each account and which
   sources are assigned to it. The load is visible before the start, so it can be rebalanced.

## Why the module only takes fresh posts

On start it records what has already been published in the channel and then waits for new posts,
polling the sources. It deliberately leaves older ones alone: a comment under a post that has just
gone out sits at the top of the discussion and catches readers, while a reply under yesterday's post
sinks down the thread.

<Aside type="caution" title="A practical consequence">
If your sources publish rarely, a short observation window will end **without a single comment**.
That is not a failure — it means there were no matching posts.
</Aside>

## How to write the prompt

- Describe who is writing and why they read this channel — the reply comes out meaningful
  rather than neutral.
- Ask for one or two sentences strictly on the topic of the post: long comments look unnatural.
- Explicitly forbid filler like “great” and “thanks for the post” — that is exactly what gives a bot away.
- Do not ask for links: comments with links collect complaints fastest.

## A safe test run

<Steps>

1. Take **two warmed-up accounts** and **one channel of your own** with open comments.
2. Set selection to “All matching” and the calmest pace profile.
3. Publish a test post in the channel and wait for the comment.
4. Read the text in the log: it should read like a human reply on the topic of the post.

</Steps>

This checks account access, model behaviour and prompt quality at once — without loading
anyone else's channels.

## Why a target may be skipped

- No new posts appeared in the source during the observation window.
- The post did not match the keywords or fall into the random share.
- The account hit its daily action limit or is waiting out a pause between comments.
- The model did not respond or returned empty text — no comment is sent.
- Telegram restricted the action, or comments are closed on the target.

The reason for each target is visible in the run results. Common scenarios are covered
in [troubleshooting](/en/troubleshooting/).

## How the module protects accounts

The pace profile decides how fast an account works: the calmer the profile, the longer the pauses
between comments, and every pause differs slightly from the previous one. The daily action limit
and the night break are configured separately. If Telegram asks to wait (FloodWait), the thread
pauses instead of pushing the account further.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Launching on accounts that were not warmed up | restrictions arrive fastest |
| Too many channels per account | the extras simply are not picked up |
| Identical text from every account | obvious to readers and to anti-spam alike |
| Links in comments | the shortest path to complaints and restrictions |

## Limits, user consent and platform rules

Commenting takes place on someone else's ground, so it is worth settling the boundaries in advance:

- Comment only where the channel owner does not object to such activity. Open discussions
  are not consent to automated comments.
- Telegram's rules and the rules of the specific community take precedence over panel settings.
- The module does not bypass Telegram's restrictions: if comments are closed or the account is
  limited, the panel shows it rather than forcing through.
- Responsibility for the content of comments lies with the operator — an instruction to a model
  does not remove that.

See [Responsible use](/en/responsible-use/).

## FAQ

**Does the module comment on old posts?**
No. On start it records what has already been published and then waits only for new posts.

**What if the model does not respond?**
No comment is sent and the reason stays in the results. A template placeholder is never substituted
for a model reply, so one provider error does not turn into a series of identical comments.

**Can two accounts comment on the same post?**
No. Within one job a post goes to a single account.

**Is Telegram Premium required?**
Not for ordinary work. Premium is only needed for the comment to appear on behalf of a channel
linked to the account.

**Can it work without an AI provider?**
Yes, in template mode. AI and mixed modes require access to a model.

**Is it obvious that a model writes the comments?**
It depends on the instruction. Generic phrases, identical text and off-topic replies give
automation away.
