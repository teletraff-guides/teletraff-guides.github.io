The module places reactions in your targets: it either walks through already published messages
or keeps targets under observation and reacts to new ones as they appear.

The module overview lives on the
[mass reactions page](https://teletraff.com/en/modules/mass-reactions).

## What you need before launch

- **Accounts with proxies**, preferably [warmed up](/en/modules/account-warmup/).
- **A list of channels or chats** — entered manually or as a ready dataset.

## How to launch

<Steps>

1. **Choose the scenario.**

   | Scenario | What it does |
   | --- | --- |
   | From a ready list | accounts walk through your targets and react to existing messages |
   | Watch for new | the module keeps targets under observation and reacts to fresh messages |

2. **Set the emoji set and the probability.**

   The set can be anything — the panel picks from your selection in random order.

   <figure>
     <img src="/screenshots/mass-reactions-setup.webp" alt="TeleTraff mass reactions setup screen with emoji set, probability, limits and thread distribution" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
     <figcaption>Settings: reaction set, probability, limits and thread distribution across accounts.</figcaption>
   </figure>

3. **Set limits and rotation.**

   - **Reactions per target** — how many an account places in the current channel before moving on.
   - **Total reaction limit** — the ceiling for the whole run.
   - **Rotation** — an account gives way to the next after a set number of reactions, so one does
     not consume the whole limit.
   - **Join the target** — a separate toggle for when Telegram requires membership to react.
     Without that requirement the account joins nothing.

4. **Start it and watch the result.**

</Steps>

## Why reactions are not placed on everything

Reaction probability is the main naturalness control.

<Aside type="caution" title="One hundred per cent is the clearest sign of automation">
At one hundred per cent an account marks every message in a row, and from the outside it looks
exactly like what it is: mechanical placement. A value noticeably below one hundred gives a lifelike
picture — some messages stay without a reaction, like with an ordinary reader. Tune it on a small run.
</Aside>

## Reactions on behalf of a channel

A separate mode: the reaction comes from the account's personal channel rather than the account itself.

- Telegram Premium on the account and its personal channel are required.
- The panel shows in advance how many selected accounts meet the conditions.
- Accounts without Premium simply do not take part in this mode.

## A safe test run

<Steps>

1. Take **two accounts** and **a channel of your own** with open reactions.
2. Lower the probability well below one hundred and set small limits — per target and total.
3. Run it and read the log: some messages should stay without a reaction.
4. Check that the reactions vary rather than repeating one emoji.

</Steps>

## Why a reaction may not have gone through

- Reactions are disabled for everyone on the target.
- Reactions are forbidden for this account there.
- Telegram's own reaction limit was reached.
- Telegram requires joining the chat and joining is turned off.
- Telegram asked to wait (FloodWait) — the thread pauses.

Refusals appear in the log with a reason: it is immediately clear whether the target or the account
is at fault.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| One hundred per cent probability | the clearest sign of automation |
| The whole total limit on one account | the load is not spread across the fleet |
| Targets where reactions are closed | the run does nothing |

## Limits and platform rules

Reactions are a public action in someone else's community. Place them where such activity is
appropriate, and remember that Telegram's rules and the specific channel's rules take precedence
over job settings. See [Responsible use](/en/responsible-use/).

## FAQ

**How many reactions can one run place?**
The total run limit and the per-target limit are set with sliders, and the panel shows the ceiling
next to them. Keep both well below it: the point is evenness, not volume.

**Why did some reactions fail?**
Common causes: reactions are off on the target, forbidden for the account there, Telegram's limit
was reached, or the account is not in the chat. The exact reason is in the results.

**Is Telegram Premium required?**
Not for ordinary reactions. Premium is only needed for the personal-channel mode.

**What probability should I set?**
Noticeably below one hundred. The exact value is tuned on a small run for your topic;
one hundred per cent is best avoided entirely.

**What is rotation for?**
So one account does not consume the whole limit: after a set number of reactions it gives way
to the next.

**What does joining the target do?**
If Telegram will not accept a reaction from a non-member, the account joins first and then reacts.
