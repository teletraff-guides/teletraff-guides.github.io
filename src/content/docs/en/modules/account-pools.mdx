A pool is a saved group of accounts you can select in a job as a whole, instead of rebuilding
the selection every time.

The section overview lives on the [account pools page](https://teletraff.com/en/modules/account-pools).

## Why it matters

With a small fleet a selection takes a minute to assemble by hand. With dozens of accounts, manual
assembly becomes a source of mistakes: an account that is warming up ends up in a job, or one with
a different proxy profile does.

<Aside type="tip" title="The practical point">
A pool fixes the **composition**, not just convenience. “Commenters”, “inviters”, “reserve” are
different groups with different histories and different risk. Mixing them in one job is rarely
a good idea.
</Aside>

## How to build one

<Steps>

1. **Select accounts in the [account manager](/en/modules/account-manager/).**

   Select by purpose: accounts for one type of task should have comparable preparation.

2. **Save them as a pool** and give it a clear name.

   <figure>
     <img src="/screenshots/account-pools.webp" alt="TeleTraff account pools section with saved groups of accounts and their composition" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
     <figcaption>Account pools: saved groups and their composition for reuse in jobs.</figcaption>
   </figure>

3. **Select the pool when configuring a job** instead of picking accounts by hand.

4. **Keep the composition current.** An account that has been restricted or sent to warmup should
   leave the working pool — otherwise it will keep entering jobs and being filtered out at the start.

</Steps>

## How to split a fleet

There is no universal scheme, but a working principle is to split by type of load:

| Pool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Commenters | [AI commenting](/en/modules/neurocommenting/), [AI chat](/en/modules/ai-chat/) |
| Inviters | [inviting](/en/modules/inviting/) — accounts with rights in the target channels |
| Senders | [messaging](/en/modules/messaging/) — the heaviest load |
| Reserve | warms up and waits to replace accounts that drop out |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| One pool for every task | loads of different types fall on the same accounts |
| A restricted account left in a working pool | it enters jobs and is filtered out at the start |
| A pool with no reserve | there is nobody to replace a lost account, and job volume falls |

## FAQ

**Can an account belong to several pools?**
A pool's composition is visible in the section, and one account may be needed by different
scenarios. Just remember it runs one job at a time.

**What happens to a pool if an account gets restricted?**
It stays in the composition but will not go into work. Composition is worth maintaining by hand.

**Does a pool replace filters in the account manager?**
No. Filters are convenient for a one-off selection; a pool is for the one you need regularly.
