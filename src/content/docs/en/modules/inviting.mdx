The inviter invites people from a collected dataset into your channel or group. Before launch
the panel checks whether each selected account can actually invite into that target.

The module overview lives on the [inviter page](https://teletraff.com/en/modules/inviter).

## What you need before launch

- **Accounts with proxies.** Without a proxy an account will not run.
- **A dataset of people, not channels.** The inviter invites people — results of
  [parsing](/en/modules/parsers/) members or commenters will do.
- **A target your accounts can already invite into.** The panel does not grant or revoke admin
  rights — you do that inside Telegram.
- **Readiness for the fact that some people will not join** for reasons outside the panel's
  control — their privacy settings, for example.

## How to launch

<Steps>

1. **Select the dataset, accounts and target.**

   The target can be an existing channel or group — the panel will check your accounts' access.
   Alternatively you can create a new channel: the panel creates a real one with one of your
   accounts and only uses it after a confirmed result. The target can be changed right up to
   launch; after a change the permission check runs again.

   <figure>
     <img src="/screenshots/inviter-setup.webp" alt="TeleTraff inviter setup screen with accounts, a dataset of users, the target channel and two limits" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
     <figcaption>Settings: inviter accounts, the user dataset, the target channel, two limits and thread distribution.</figcaption>
   </figure>

2. **Wait for the permission check.**

   The check runs on **every** selected account, not a sample. Until at least one account is
   confirmed, the launch button stays disabled — so the job never starts on accounts whose rights
   are unknown.

   The check runs in batches, so a large selection takes longer. There is no cap on the number
   of accounts: you wait longer, but the selection is not trimmed.

3. **Set the two limits — and see what they actually produce.**

   “Total invitations” is the ceiling for the whole job. “Maximum per account” is the ceiling for
   one account. Whichever runs out first is the one that applies.

   <Aside type="caution" title="The real volume">
   The actual number of actions equals the **smaller** of the two: the total limit, and
   “maximum per account × number of accounts”.

   If the total limit is generous but the per-account one is modest, the plan will come out
   noticeably below the total. The panel shows the actual figure in the thread distribution block
   before the start.
   </Aside>

4. **Set the pace and the FloodWait policy.**

   Three pause presets between invites — fast, balanced and cautious; the panel shows the range
   in seconds next to each. Any custom value switches the mode to “Custom”. The pause always
   carries a spread: consecutive actions never repeat the same interval.

   <figure>
     <img src="/screenshots/inviter-protection.webp" alt="Inviter pace settings with pause presets, a custom value and the FloodWait policy" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
     <figcaption>Pace: pause presets, a custom value with spread, the FloodWait policy and a per-account time estimate.</figcaption>
   </figure>

   If Telegram returns a FloodWait, there are two responses: **wait and continue** or **stop this
   account** for the current job. The other accounts keep working. An “extra pause” adds your own
   minutes on top of the delay Telegram named.

5. **Start it and watch the threads.**

   <figure>
     <img src="/screenshots/inviter-threads.webp" alt="Inviter runs tab with one line per account, counters and an event log" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
     <figcaption>Runs: a separate line per account, counters and an event log by account and target.</figcaption>
   </figure>

</Steps>

## Batch processing

Batch processing splits an account's work into parts: it sends a set number of invitations, then
pauses for a number of hours and continues. This helps when the dataset is large and you want
to stretch it over a day or more.

The pause is visible in the log as “the account reached its batch size and will continue after
the configured pause”, so silence in a thread never has to be guessed at.

## How to read the results

<figure>
  <img src="/screenshots/inviter-results.webp" alt="Inviter results tab broken down by successes, skips, blocks and errors" width="1264" height="1080" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Results: finished jobs broken down into successes, skips, blocks, deferred and errors.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Success | the person was added and Telegram confirmed it. This also covers people already in the channel |
| Skipped | it never got as far as sending: a duplicate in the dataset, an exhausted limit or a stopped account |
| Blocked | protection stopped the action before contacting Telegram |
| Deferred | Telegram limited the attempt and the target was not closed in this run |
| Errors | Telegram refused, or membership was not confirmed after the request |

Deferred targets are what give a job the “Partial” status, even when there are few errors.

## Why a person may not have joined

- **Their privacy settings forbid being added to groups and channels.** This is the most common
  reason and it cannot be bypassed.
- They are already in the channel — the panel checks before sending, so no limit is spent.
- Telegram accepted the request but membership was not confirmed on re-check — this is recorded
  separately and never passed off as success.
- The account hit a FloodWait or an anti-spam warning and stopped in this job.
- The target appeared in the dataset twice — a repeat invitation is not sent.

## A safe test run

<Steps>

1. Take **two warmed-up accounts** and **a channel of your own** where they can invite.
2. Build a dataset of **10–15 people** and choose the cautious pace preset.
3. Run it and wait for completion.
4. Open the results: the share of closed profiles shows the real quality of that dataset source.

</Steps>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Launching on accounts that were not warmed up or rested | fresh sessions get restricted fastest |
| Checking permissions on one account and launching on ten | only the confirmed ones will work |
| Setting the total limit by eye, ignoring the per-account one | the plan comes out far smaller than expected |
| Judging a dataset by one run | the share of closed profiles differs several-fold between sources |

## Limits, user consent and platform rules

An invitation adds a person to a community without their explicit request, so boundaries matter:

- Invite people to whom your channel is genuinely relevant. Presence in a collected dataset
  **is not consent** to being added.
- A user's privacy settings are their decision, and the panel does not work around them.
- Telegram's rules take precedence over job settings; mass invitations can lead to account
  restrictions.
- You are responsible for the content of the channel you invite people into.

See [Responsible use](/en/responsible-use/).

## FAQ

**Why is the launch button disabled?**
Usually the permission check has not covered every selected account yet. The hint under the button
names what is missing.

**Is a limit spent on people already in the channel?**
No. The panel checks membership before contacting Telegram, and such a person counts as a success
without an invitation being sent.

**What happens to the targets of an account that hit a FloodWait?**
Those it did not reach are handed to another free account.

**Can I invite people into someone else's channel?**
No. The selected accounts must already have the right to invite into that target.

**What pace is safe?**
There is no single threshold. Start with the cautious preset on a small volume and watch how
Telegram responds.

**Can I stop one account without touching the others?**
Yes. Pause and stop apply to an individual line.
